General Crop-Soil Suitability (Widely-Known Reference, Not a Substitute for Local Expert Advice)

Different soil types traditionally suit different crops. These are general, widely-known associations - actual suitability also depends on climate, season, rainfall, and local conditions, so this should be treated as a starting point for discussion with a local agricultural officer, not a final recommendation.

Black soil (regur soil): commonly associated with cotton cultivation in India, due to its high water-retention capacity. Also suitable for soybean, sorghum (jowar), and sugarcane in adequate rainfall areas.

Sandy soil: drains quickly, generally suited to crops tolerant of lower water retention, such as groundnut, millets (bajra), and certain vegetables, when irrigation is managed carefully and frequently.

Clay soil: retains water well but drains slowly; suited to crops that tolerate more consistent moisture, such as paddy/rice (especially heavy clay that holds standing water) and wheat in many regions.

Loamy soil: generally considered good all-purpose soil with balanced drainage and water retention, suited to a wide range of crops including vegetables, wheat, and many cash crops.

Red soil: typically lower in fertility than black soil, but with proper fertilization, supports crops like groundnut, millets, and pulses in many regions.

Season also matters significantly: in India, kharif crops (sown with monsoon rains, roughly June-October) include rice, cotton, soybean, and maize; rabi crops (sown after monsoon, roughly October-March) include wheat, mustard, and gram; zaid crops (short summer season) include some vegetables and fodder crops.

Because actual crop choice depends on soil type, season, local rainfall, market access, and the farmer's specific land and resources, this information should be treated as a general starting point. A local agricultural extension officer or Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) can give a recommendation suited to the specific field and current season.
