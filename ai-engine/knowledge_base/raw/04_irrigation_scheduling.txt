Irrigation Scheduling

Irrigation scheduling is deciding when and how much to irrigate. Good scheduling balances three factors: current soil moisture, upcoming weather (especially rain chance), and the crop's current growth stage.

General guidance for scheduling decisions:
- If soil moisture is already adequate and rain is expected soon, irrigation can usually be delayed or skipped to avoid overwatering.
- If soil moisture is low and no rain is expected, irrigation is generally advisable.
- If soil moisture is low but rain is expected within a day, a farmer may choose to wait and observe, since rainfall may resolve the deficit — though this depends on how reliable the forecast is and how stressed the crop currently looks.

Early morning or evening irrigation is generally preferred over midday, since it reduces water loss to evaporation and reduces heat stress on the plant during watering.

Scheduling decisions should always be treated as guidance to combine with a farmer's own field observation, not as an automatic instruction.
