Crop-Specific Irrigation Guidance (Reference Only)

How to use this file: each crop below has its own paragraph with approximate seasonal water need, the growth stages when water stress hurts yield most, and when to reduce or stop irrigation. Figures are general ranges from FAO guidance and common Indian package-of-practice recommendations; they vary with variety, soil, season and local climate. Confirm with the local agricultural extension office or Krishi Vigyan Kendra before relying on them. Sources: FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 33 (Yield Response to Water), FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 56 (Crop Evapotranspiration), FAO Irrigation Water Management Training Manual 3 (Brouwer and Heibloem).

Wheat irrigation guidance: total seasonal water need is roughly 450 to 650 mm (FAO-33). The most important growth stages for water are crown root initiation (about 3 weeks after sowing), tillering, jointing, flowering and grain filling (milk and dough stage). Water stress at crown root initiation and at flowering and grain filling causes the biggest yield loss. Wheat normally needs about 4 to 6 irrigations in total, spaced by these stages. Reduce and stop irrigation at the hard-dough stage, roughly 2 weeks before harvest. Avoid waterlogging. Comfortable soil moisture range for wheat is about 25 to 45 percent on this system.

Rice and paddy irrigation guidance: rice needs standing water for much of its cycle. Crop water use is roughly 450 to 700 mm (FAO-33), but total field water including land preparation and percolation losses is often 1,000 to 1,500 mm. Most sensitive stages are transplanting and establishment, panicle initiation, booting and flowering. Keep about 2 to 5 cm of standing water in the vegetative stage and keep the field wet at flowering. Alternate wetting and drying can save water: irrigate again when the water level in a field tube falls about 15 cm below the soil surface, but not during flowering. Drain the field about 10 to 15 days before harvest. Rice tolerates saturated soil, so a high sensor moisture reading is normal for paddy.

Cotton irrigation guidance: seasonal water need is roughly 700 to 1,300 mm (FAO-33), and cotton is fairly drought tolerant early on. Water stress hurts most during flowering, boll formation and boll development. Too much water in the vegetative stage causes excess leaf growth and weak boll set. Reduce irrigation as bolls begin to open and stop when most bolls have opened. Avoid waterlogging because cotton roots are sensitive to prolonged saturation. Comfortable soil moisture range for cotton is about 25 to 40 percent on this system. Black cotton soils hold water for a long time, so irrigate less often but with care.

Soybean irrigation guidance: seasonal water need is roughly 450 to 700 mm (FAO-33). Adequate moisture is needed for germination and emergence. The most sensitive stages are flowering, pod setting and pod filling (seed filling), where water stress cuts yield the most. Reduce and stop irrigation when leaves turn yellow and pods mature. Soybean dislikes waterlogging, especially early. Comfortable soil moisture range for soybean is about 30 to 50 percent on this system.

Maize irrigation guidance: seasonal water need is roughly 500 to 800 mm (FAO-33). The most critical period is tasseling and silking (about 2 weeks around flowering); stress then causes poor pollination and large yield loss. Grain filling is the next most sensitive stage, and seedling establishment also needs steady moisture. Reduce irrigation at the dough and dent stages and stop near physiological maturity. Maize is sensitive to waterlogging, particularly in young plants.

Tomato irrigation guidance: seasonal water need is roughly 400 to 600 mm (FAO-33). Keep moisture consistent: the most sensitive stages are flowering, fruit set and fruit enlargement. Irregular watering (dry spells followed by heavy irrigation) causes blossom-end rot and fruit cracking. Reduce water slightly as fruits ripen to limit cracking and improve quality. Drip irrigation suits tomato well and keeps leaves dry, which reduces disease. Comfortable soil moisture range for tomato is about 30 to 50 percent on this system.

Chili irrigation guidance: seasonal water need is roughly 600 to 900 mm for pepper crops (FAO-33). Chili needs steady, moderate moisture. The most sensitive stages are flowering and fruit set, where water stress causes flower and fruit drop. Waterlogging causes root rot and wilting, so do not over-irrigate. Reduce water slightly during ripening and picking. Comfortable soil moisture range for chili is about 30 to 50 percent on this system.

Other common vegetables irrigation guidance: shallow-rooted vegetables such as onion, cabbage, cauliflower and leafy greens need frequent, light irrigation instead of rare heavy irrigation. Onion should stop receiving water about 10 to 15 days before harvest, when the tops start to fall. Potato is most sensitive at tuber initiation and tuber bulking, and irrigation should stop about 10 to 14 days before harvest so the skin hardens. Brinjal and okra behave like tomato: keep moisture consistent, especially at flowering and fruit set.

General rule for growth stages: water stress is most damaging at germination and establishment, at flowering and fruit or pod setting, and during grain or fruit filling. Irrigation is usually reduced during early vegetative growth in drought-tolerant crops, and reduced or stopped near maturity and harvest. If the crop or its growth stage is not known, ask the farmer which crop and which stage before giving crop-specific advice.

Crop coefficient reference (FAO-56, mid-season, approximate): wheat 1.15, rice 1.05 to 1.20, cotton 1.15 to 1.20, soybean 1.15, maize 1.20, tomato 1.15, chili and pepper 1.05. A higher coefficient means higher daily water use at that stage.
