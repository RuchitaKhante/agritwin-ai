General Pest Management Principles (Reference Only - Not a Pesticide Recommendation)

Pest and disease management decisions depend on correctly identifying the specific pest or disease, which requires visual inspection or expert diagnosis - something a soil-moisture chatbot cannot do reliably from a text description alone.

General, safe principles that apply broadly:
- Integrated Pest Management (IPM) favors prevention first: crop rotation, resistant varieties, and field sanitation reduce pest pressure before chemical intervention is needed.
- Correct pest identification matters - the same symptom (e.g. leaf holes, yellowing) can have different causes requiring different treatments; treating the wrong pest wastes money and can harm the crop or beneficial insects.
- Pesticide application timing matters for both effectiveness and safety - many pesticides should not be applied during flowering (to protect pollinators) or shortly before harvest (for residue safety).
- Always follow the product label exactly - dosage, safety equipment, and re-entry/harvest intervals specified on a pesticide label are based on safety testing and must be followed precisely.
- Overuse of pesticides can lead to pest resistance over time, making future infestations harder to control.

Because correct pesticide selection and dosage depends on accurately identifying the specific pest, local regulations, and the product label, this chatbot cannot and should not recommend a specific pesticide product or dosage. For pest or disease problems, consult your local agricultural extension officer, a licensed pesticide dealer, or a Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK), who can correctly identify the issue and recommend an appropriate, safe treatment.
