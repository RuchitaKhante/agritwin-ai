Irrigation Basics

Irrigation means supplying water to crops through means other than natural rainfall, such as sprinklers, drip lines, or flood channels. The goal is to keep soil moisture within the range a crop needs to grow well, without over-watering or under-watering.

Over-irrigation can lead to waterlogging, root rot, nutrient leaching, and wasted water. Under-irrigation causes wilting, stunted growth, and reduced yield. Both extremes stress the plant, so the aim is consistency rather than maximum water.

Common irrigation methods include drip irrigation (water delivered slowly near the root zone, water-efficient), sprinkler irrigation (water sprayed over the field, good for larger areas), and flood/furrow irrigation (water flows across the field, simple but less water-efficient).

The right irrigation amount and frequency depends on crop type, growth stage, soil type, and current weather. A single fixed schedule rarely works well across an entire season.
