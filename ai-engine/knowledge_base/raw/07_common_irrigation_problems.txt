Common Irrigation Problems

Overwatering: Symptoms include yellowing lower leaves, soft/mushy roots, fungal growth at the soil surface, and a persistently waterlogged look to the soil. Overwatering can lead to root rot, which weakens the plant's ability to take up nutrients even when water is available.

Underwatering: Symptoms include wilting (especially during the hottest part of the day), dry and curling leaf edges, slowed growth, and soil that pulls away from the edges of a pot or bed.

Uneven irrigation: Some areas of a field receiving more water than others, often due to uneven land leveling or clogged drip emitters, can cause patchy crop growth. Regularly checking irrigation equipment for clogs or leaks helps prevent this.

Yellowing leaves specifically can have multiple causes — overwatering, nutrient deficiency, or disease — and soil moisture data alone cannot always distinguish between them. When yellowing appears alongside normal or low soil moisture, nutrient deficiency or disease should be suspected, and consulting a local agricultural expert is advisable for a confident diagnosis.
